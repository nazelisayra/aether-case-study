Overview

Making Aether easy to follow

Aether made precision parts with its own software and automated factories. The old website had the information, but it was hard to follow.

I was the only designer and handled research, structure, visuals and the front end.

Two audiences, one site

Most competitors sell fast parts to engineers and buyers. Aether also wanted traditional US factories to run on its OS and join its network, so it could grow without building every plant itself.

Part buyers were the core customers, so they came first. The vision still had to be clear for future partners.

Sole designer, three months, no brand system, and a company that split in two near the end.

Decision 01

Draw the vision as a blueprint

Aether's edge was its Fabricator cell and a franchise model for factories. Stock photos couldn't show that, so I drew it like a technical drawing: one factory in Erie, PA, copied across the US and abroad.

The founders saw it as a visionary step that set Aether apart.

Aether blueprint

The same drawing language, inside the product story

I carried the blueprint into the quote-to-delivery flow. Production, the step buyers can't see, is drawn with technically accurate machining views instead of a stock factory photo.

Decision 02

Build the hierarchy around buyers' questions

With the vision set, the rest of the site had to be easy to act on. Each page answers one question, and the details follow in the order buyers look for them.

After the split

Carried into Beskar

When Aether's founders split, the team moved to Beskar and kept Technology and Services. I then designed an Industries menu and five sector pages built around real parts.

Beskar's developer shipped them and has since added two more sectors on the same template.

Reflection

Keeping what the company does separate from its story let the work survive the company changing.

The Aether home page told that story, from one factory in Erie to a network. It didn't make the move to Beskar, but its blueprint language lives on in every page that did.
